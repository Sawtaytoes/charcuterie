# The token layer

Three axes, all `<html>` data attributes, all composing:

| Attribute | Values | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-scheme` | `light` · `dark` | colour only |
| `data-density` | `comfortable` · `compact` · `kiosk` | control sizing + type scale |
| `data-variant` | `daylight` · `hairline` · `layered` · `legible` | the visual direction |

Switch any of them in the toolbar above. **Nothing in React observes the change** —
that is the whole design. One attribute flip re-themes every component with zero
re-render, because components name semantic roles (`surface.raised`,
`intent.danger.solid`) and the roles resolve through CSS custom properties.

If a component ever needs a `useTheme()` hook to respond correctly, the token layer
has failed, and this toolbar is where it shows up first.

## ePaper is not a fourth axis

It is a separate export, `@charcuterie/tokens/epaper`, because it *removes*
capabilities rather than restyling them: no hover, no opacity, no shadow, no
transition, and a fixed six-colour palette the panel can physically render.
Modelling it as `data-scheme="epaper"` would imply a `data-variant` still applies to
it, which it cannot.

## Defaults

`daylight` won the M0 bake-off and is the default `data-variant`. The default
*scheme* stays **dark**, deliberately — picking a light-first direction is not the
same as flipping the fleet to light, and the kiosk Pis stay pinned to dark. A light
kiosk in a dark room is a lamp.

The three losing directions are live alternates rather than deleted files. That they
cost nothing to keep is the payoff for generating the bake-off board from token files
instead of drawing it.

## So when would you actually use `hairline`, `layered`, or `legible`?

Short version: **most of the time you would not — you want `data-density`.** Each of the
three was built for a viewing condition rather than a taste, and each one names the
condition it is for. Flip the Variant toolbar against `Tokens/Specimen` to see them.

| Direction | The condition it is for | Reach for it when | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hairline` | **Reading a lot of rows at desk distance.** Separation is a 1px border and a small surface step, never a shadow; motion is 120ms and flat, so the UI feels immediate. | A surface that is mostly a list — ripdeck's 16-bay tower, mux-magic's sequence log, a plex-channels queue — and `data-density="compact"` on `daylight` still feels loose. | Anything read from further than a desk. Hairlines stop existing at kiosk distance, and they are the *only* separation this direction has. |
| `layered` | **Being looked at from across a room.** Separation is a real surface step plus a shadow, radii are 12–20px, motion is 200–320ms with overshoot, accent is warm. | The kiosk Pi and xander — a finger or a remote, two to three metres away. Pair it with `data-density="kiosk"`; it is the same bet at a different layer. | A dense list (it eats vertical space, so fewer rows fit) and anything ePaper — shadow-based separation collapses to nothing on a panel with no greys to spare. |
| `legible` | **Bad conditions, or a person who needs the headroom.** Every content-on-surface pair targets WCAG AAA (7:1) instead of AA, intents are pushed until they differ at a glance, borders are drawn rather than implied, and the focus ring is 3px. | The garage tablet at 2am with a rip failing, a display in direct sun, or a user-facing "high contrast" preference. It is the honest thing to put behind that switch. | A long working session. Everything at 7:1 with saturated status colour is tiring, and when every state shouts, none of them does. |

`legible` has a second, permanent job that has nothing to do with anyone choosing it:
it is the **upper bound `contrast.test.ts` measures `daylight` against**. Deleting it
would remove the benchmark, which is most of why the three losers were kept.

**None of the three is the answer for ePaper.** That is
`@charcuterie/tokens/epaper`, for the reason in the section above.

### The trap: it is one axis, and the three attributes have to travel together

`data-variant` is realistically an `<html>`-level choice. Setting it on a subtree
*half*-applies the direction, because the generated selectors pair it with the other two
axes — `[data-variant="hairline"][data-scheme="dark"]`, not a bare
`[data-variant="hairline"]`. So a `<section data-variant="hairline">` inside a
`daylight`/`dark` page picks up hairline's **radii** and keeps daylight's **colours and
control heights**:

```html
<!-- half a theme: 7px radii, daylight's #1D2430 surface, daylight's 2.5rem controls -->
<section data-variant="hairline">…</section>

<!-- the whole direction: 7px radii, #15171A surface, 2.25rem controls -->
<section data-variant="hairline" data-scheme="dark" data-density="comfortable">…</section>
```

Nothing errors either way, which is what makes it a trap. If you only want a denser
list, `data-density="compact"` **is** per-surface and needs no companions — that is the
axis to reach for, and it is what the `daylight` decision record already points at for
ripdeck's bay list.

## Contrast is a test, not a guideline

`packages/tokens/src/contrast.test.ts` walks every content-on-surface and intent pair
across every (variant x scheme) and fails below threshold.

- **WCAG 2.2 is the gate** — 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for control boundaries and focus
  indicators. Normative, and what an audit will use.
- **APCA is reported alongside** — it models perceived contrast far better on dark
  UI, but WCAG 3 is still a working draft, so gating on it means gating on a moving
  target.

Two categories are reported and never gated, each for a stated reason: disabled
content (WCAG exempts inactive controls) and decorative lines. Getting that scoping
wrong is not harmless — gating decoration at 3:1 produced 65 "failures" on the first
run, which is precisely how a contrast gate gets switched off.
