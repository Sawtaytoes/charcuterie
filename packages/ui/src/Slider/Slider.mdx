# Slider

A single-value range control: what `<input type="range">` is, without the OS painting it.

The fleet had no way to ask for a value along a range, so QueuePilot's Now-playing bar
could not have a scrubber and the choice was a native range input or a hand-rolled one in
a single app. `ProgressBar` looks like the answer and is not — it is **output**, it takes
no input, and `role="progressbar"` tells assistive technology exactly that.

## Why a `div` and not `<input type="range">`

The same reason [`Select` is deprecated](?path=/docs/components-controls-select--docs).

A range input's thumb and track are UA pseudo-elements — `::-webkit-slider-thumb`,
`::-moz-range-track` — and they are not one surface: the two engines disagree about which
of them the filled portion even belongs to, so a token-styled range is two stylesheets that
drift apart. Windows paints its own regardless.

`role="slider"` on a focusable `div` is the APG's own pattern, gets the same AT support, and
every pixel of it is ours.

## The role, the tab stop and the pointer target are all the track

Putting them on the thumb is what a hand-rolled slider usually does, because the thumb is
the part that looks draggable. It gives the widget a 16px box: a press on the bar does
nothing, and a Playwright bounding box is the handle rather than the control.

The hit area is a row taller than the bar, because a 2px track is not a pointer target.

## Drag does not commit

`onChange` fires on every movement — each arrow key, each pointer sample. `onChangeEnd`
fires once, on release or key up.

This split exists because the expensive consumer of a slider is almost never the paint. A
seek scrubber issuing its network call from `onChange` would send one request per pointer
sample and arrive at the right offset **last**, behind a queue of stale ones.

While a drag is in flight the component paints from its own local value rather than from
the `value` prop. Without that, a controlled slider whose owner only commits on
`onChangeEnd` would render the thumb pinned at the old position under the finger — the drag
would look broken while behaving correctly.

## Read-only is not disabled

A read-only slider keeps full contrast and stays focusable, so its value is still readable
and still reachable by a screen reader. A live stream's position is this: the number
matters and seeking is not offered.

## The arithmetic is a tested pure function

`sliderValue.ts`, in the node project. Steps anchor on `min` rather than on zero — a 7–19
range stepping by 4 anchored on zero can reach 8, 12 and 16, and neither end of its own
range is one of them. A snap never escapes the range, a float step does not accumulate
drift into `aria-valuenow`, and a `NaN` cannot reach the style attribute, where it would
render `inline-size: NaN%` and paint nothing with no error.

`fromFraction` takes an `isRtl` flag because `getBoundingClientRect()` is physical and knows
nothing about writing direction — without it a right-to-left slider runs backwards under the
pointer while its keyboard path stays correct.
