# PortraitTiles

A set of **people** — or of anything else picked by its face — drawn as round pictures with a
name and one big number.

points-market's "Who's shopping?" is the shape this is taken from. It had been hand-rolled
there since the app was built, and it is the third tile shape the fleet grew on its own,
after mux-magic's and Gallery Downloader's "Pick a tool" cards.

## Why this is not an `ActionTiles` variant

The two answer different questions.

- [`ActionTiles`](/docs/components-actions-actiontiles--docs) asks **what do you want to
  do** — a name, a line of help, an icon that illustrates a verb.
- This asks **who is this** — a face, and one number that decides it. There is no
  description, because a person is not explained.

Collapsing them would mean one component whose `icon` is sometimes a 20px glyph beside the
title and sometimes a 144px circle above it, with `hint` meaning "what this does" in one mode
and "points" in the other. That is two components wearing one name.

What they **do** share is the box: `tileStyles.ts` holds the border, the radius, the surface
and the `auto-fill` grid, and both read it. A page carrying both does not show two different
cards.

## It scales, and the scaling is the point

Two axes carry it, and neither is a pixel count in an app.

**The container.** `layout="auto"` is a row while the set is narrow and a column once it has
room, off a `cq-sm` (24rem) query on the set itself. Nothing reads the viewport, so a picker
in a 320px sidebar on a 2560px monitor gets the row form — which is right, and which a media
query would have got wrong.

**The density.** Every length is a token, so `data-density="kiosk"` grows the type ramp
underneath it: `text-2xl` is 30px at `comfortable` and 38px at `kiosk`. points-market runs
`kiosk` today, and that is where its big numbers come from.

<Canvas of={PortraitTilesStories.Responsive} />

The container and the element querying it **cannot be the same box** — a container query
matches descendants only. The set renders a wrapper for that reason, the same arrangement
`EmptyState` uses, and `className` stays on the outer element where a consumer expects it.

## The colour is the person's, not the position's

A portrait takes the next hue from the ten-wide
[categorical palette](/docs/foundations-colour--docs) when it names none, which costs a call
site nothing and is the right default for a list assembled on the fly.

It is the **wrong** default for a household. Add a fifth member and the first four change
colour — and their colour was the thing the picker was being read by, faster than the names
were. Any set whose subjects persist should store a `categorical` per subject and pass it,
the same call Docket makes for a project.

`categorical` and not `intent`: an intent is a claim the design system makes — `danger` says
what happens if you press the thing — and a person is not a warning.

### When the colour is not ours to choose

Some colours are not a design decision at all. points-market colours each child to match the
**NFC card they physically tap**, by a settled household decision, so the picker on the wall
and the card in their hand have to agree. Ten audited hues cannot promise that, and
re-theming the colour would be re-theming the card.

That is what `color` is for, and it is the same line
[`Swatch`](/docs/components-data-swatch--docs) draws: a colour that arrived from the world is
data, not a token. It reaches the paint through custom properties on the tile, because
Tailwind generates its CSS at build time from source text and a colour out of a database is
not in the source.

Three things happen to that one colour, and only the first uses it raw.

- The **fill** is the colour, exactly.
- The **initials** are black or white, whichever the fill can carry. Pass six-digit hex:
  `getReadableTextColour` measures hex and nothing else, and falls back to white on anything
  it cannot parse rather than throwing inside your render.
- The **number** is the colour mixed toward the scheme's own text colour, so a pale card
  colour is still readable on a pale surface — and, in the dark scheme, is pulled the other
  way instead.

Reach for this arm only when the colour is a **fact about the subject**. A colour that is
merely a choice is `categorical`, and it gets re-theming and a contrast guarantee for free.

## A picture, and what happens when it 404s

`imageSrc` draws a photo; `initials` draws a letter on the hue's solid fill. A portrait grid
is the shape that cannot absorb a broken image — a missing `<img>` is a torn hole where a
face was, next to three that loaded — so a load error falls back to the initials the caller
already supplied.

The `<img>` is `alt=""` on purpose. The name is beside it inside the same tile, so an alt
here is the name announced twice.

## The keyboard is the platform's

`href` makes a portrait a real `<a href>`, routed through the injected `RouterLink` for a
same-origin destination — so middle-click, ctrl-click, "open in a new tab" and "copy link
address" come from the element rather than from the paint. Without one it is a `<button>`
that calls `onChoose`. A set may mix the two.

Tab reaches **each** portrait. There is no roving tabindex, for the same reason `ActionTiles`
has none: a radio group borrows one tab stop because exactly one of its options is true at a
time, and nothing here is true.

The group takes a required `label` naming the set — "Who's shopping?" — and `stat` is drawn
inside the tile with its `hint` so both are part of the accessible name. "1,240" on its own
tells a screen-reader user nothing.

## The lift, and who does not get it

Hovering raises the portrait and casts a shadow. It is the one piece of motion in this
library, kept because it is the thing the owner pointed at in points-market when this moved
in here.

`motion-reduce:hover:translate-y-0` turns the movement off for anyone who has asked the OS
for less. The shadow and the coloured border stay, so the hover is still legible without it.

## What this deliberately does not do

- **Hold a selection.** Nothing here is checked; the page moved on. A picker that keeps a
  value is `RadioGroup itemShape="tile"`.
- **Guarantee contrast on a `color` you supply.** The palette arm is audited in both schemes;
  the data arm cannot be, because the colour is not ours. The initials are picked to suit the
  fill and the number is pulled toward readable, and that is the whole of what it can promise.
- **Crop or focus a face.** `object-fit: cover` centres, and that is all. An app that needs a
  focal point owns the crop.
