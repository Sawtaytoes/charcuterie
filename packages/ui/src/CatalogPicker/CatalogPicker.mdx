# CatalogPicker

A picker over a large catalog — forty AI models from six providers, say — that you can
**browse by group** on a rail or **search across everything** at once.

<Canvas of={CatalogPickerStories.Playground} />

## When to use it, and when not

| You have | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A short list, no typing needed | `Picker` |
| One flat list that wants a filter | `Combobox` |
| A long list that falls into groups people think in — providers, accounts, vendors — and whose rows look alike until you read the end | **`CatalogPicker`** |

`Picker` has no filter, and `Combobox` filters one flat list with no way to look at just
one part of it. A catalog of models named "Provider · Model · slug" is exactly where both
fall short: every row starts the same way, and finding one means reading all of them.
The rail answers "show me this provider's models", and the search answers "I know the
name" without making the person know the provider first.

## The shape

- **The trigger** shows the chosen item's mark and label, and is named
  `"<label>: <chosen label>"`, as `Picker`'s is. Query it as
  `getByRole("button", { name: /^Agent: / })`.
- **The rail**, on the inline start: a **Favorites** star first (only when `favorites`
  resolves to at least one item), then one button per group, in `groups` order. A group
  with no rows is left off. Each button's accessible name and tooltip is the group's
  `label`.
- **The search field** at the top of the list, then **the list** for the current tab.
  Each row shows the mark, the label, the optional `description`, a small star on a
  favorite, and a check mark on the chosen row. The star and the check are drawn in CSS,
  not typed, because a symbol character renders as nothing where the font lacks it.
- **Pinned rows** (`pinned: "start"` or `"end"`) appear at the top or bottom of every
  tab and of the search results when they match — a "Default" that belongs to no group.

## Searching

Typing searches **every item**, whatever tab is current. The match is a case-insensitive
substring on the label, the description and the group's label, and **every word must
match**: `work lumen` finds the Lumen rows of the "Contoso (Work)" account and not the
others. Results sit under one heading per group, in rail order; favorites are found under
their group, not in a section of their own. While a query is live, no rail button is
pressed, because no one group is being shown.

This is not `CommandPalette`'s word-prefix match. A model's slug (`ct-lumen-4`) and a
version number (`4.5`) are things people type the middle of.

## Opening

The panel opens on **Favorites** when the chosen value is a favorite or a pinned row;
otherwise on the **chosen row's group**; otherwise on Favorites; otherwise on the first
group. The chosen row is highlighted and **centred** in the list — the rule a `Combobox`
follows when it opens on its chosen option. Focus goes to the search field.

## Keyboard

| Where | Key | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search | ArrowDown / ArrowUp | Move the highlight, skipping disabled rows |
| Search | Home / End | First / last enabled row |
| Search | Enter | Choose the highlighted row and close |
| Search | Escape | Clear the query; on an empty query, close |
| Search | Tab / Shift+Tab | To the rail |
| Rail | ArrowDown / ArrowUp, Home / End | Move between groups (wraps) |
| Rail | Enter / Space | Show that group |
| Rail | Tab / Shift+Tab | Back to the search field |
| Rail | Escape | As in the search field |

Closing with Enter or Escape returns focus to the trigger.

## The ARIA shape, and why the rail is not a `tablist`

The trigger opens a `role="dialog"` named by the trigger. The search field is a
`role="combobox"` that keeps focus and moves `aria-activedescendant` through a
`role="listbox"` — the `Combobox` contract — and search results are labelled
`role="group"`s inside that listbox.

The rail looks like tabs but is a vertical **`role="toolbar"` of `aria-pressed`
buttons**. A tablist has to have a selected tab, and while a search runs none of them is
true; and a tab controls a tab panel, while what the rail changes is a listbox that the
search field drives too. A toolbar of toggle buttons says what is true — switches that
change what the list shows — and brings the APG keyboard contract with it: one tab stop,
the arrows move, Enter and Space press.

A mouse click on a rail button does not take focus from the search field, so a person can
click a group and keep typing or arrowing.

## Icons, badges and accent colors

Charcuterie ships no icons. Pass the app's own provider logos as `icon` on a group (or on
one item, to override its group's). A group without an icon gets an **initials badge**:
the first letter of its first two words, so "Tailspin Labs" is "TL".

`accentColor` (any CSS color) draws a **ring** around the mark. It is how two accounts of
one provider are told apart. It is never the fill behind text or the text itself, so a
consumer's color cannot fail the contrast gate.

## Sizes and narrow windows

`size` sets the trigger and every row in the panel, as a `Combobox`'s `itemSize` sets its
search field and its options; a short window steps the rows down on its own. The panel is
26rem wide and 28rem tall, capped to the space the window leaves, so on a 384px phone the
rail stays and the list narrows beside it.

## Not windowed

Every row of the current view renders. `Combobox` windows only above 100 rows and the
catalogs this was built for are 40 to 60. A catalog large enough to need windowing is a
change to this component, not a prop.
