Add `LaneTimeline`: one horizontal lane per group across a date axis, with anything lasting more than a day drawn as a **bar**. Two consumers wanted the same shape — a household calendar and a task tracker's phases — and a month grid can give neither of them it: a grid breaks a multi-day thing into four cells, and every cell is the same size no matter what is in it, so a grid cannot show that one lane is carrying most of the week. A lane can, because overlapping items pack into stacked rows and a busy lane is literally taller. **`end` is inclusive** — `2026-04-06` to `2026-04-12` is seven columns, not eight — and that is stated on the prop, on the type, in the geometry module and on the docs page, because an off-by-one on a date range is invisible in both directions. The Narrow View **abandons** the axis rather than panning or squeezing it: each lane becomes one column of text with every span written out in words, and nothing scrolls sideways at any width. The fold is a **measurement of the component's own box**, not a breakpoint or a container query, because the question is how wide one *day* is — thirty columns in 900px is a readable month and three hundred and sixty five columns in the same 900px is a smear, and no query can see the second number. A zero-width item (`end` before `start`) is drawn on its start day rather than dropped, and an item clipped by either range edge keeps its own dates in its accessible name.
